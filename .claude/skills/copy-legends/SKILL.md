# COPY LEGENDS SKILL

> When writing ANY copy, follow these rules in order: (1) Diagnose awareness level, (2) Choose the right framework, (3) Write using the formulas below, (4) Validate against the quality checklist.

---

## PART 1: UNIVERSAL LAWS (Apply to ALL copy)

### Law 1 -- Know Your Prospect's Awareness Level (Eugene Schwartz)
Before writing a single word, diagnose where the prospect is:

| Level | Name | Headline Strategy | Example |
|-------|------|-------------------|---------|
| 5 | Most Aware | Direct offer, price, CTA | "Get 50% off today only" |
| 4 | Product Aware | Proof, differentiation, urgency | "Here's why [Product] beats everything else" |
| 3 | Solution Aware | Unique mechanism, superiority | "The [mechanism] method that [result] without [pain]" |
| 2 | Problem Aware | Agitate problem, introduce solution | "Struggling with [problem]? There's a reason nothing has worked" |
| 1 | Unaware | Story, curiosity, pattern interrupt | "What a retired [profession] discovered about [topic]..." |

**Rule:** The less aware the prospect, the longer and more indirect the copy must be.

### Law 2 -- Market Sophistication (Eugene Schwartz)

| Stage | Strategy |
|-------|----------|
| 1 -- Virgin market | Simple, direct claim. |
| 2 -- Enlarged claim | Bigger numbers, bolder promise. |
| 3 -- Unique mechanism | HOW it works. |
| 4 -- Expanded mechanism | Stack proof on the mechanism. |
| 5 -- Identify with prospect | Lead with identity, story, emotion. |

### Law 3 -- Enter the Conversation Already Happening (Robert Collier)
Your copy must begin where the reader's mind already IS. Use THEIR words, not yours. Research what they say at 3 AM when they can't sleep.

### Law 4 -- The Slippery Slide (Joe Sugarman)
Every element exists for ONE purpose: get the reader to read the NEXT sentence. Short first sentence (max 8 words). Curiosity in every paragraph. Open loops. Varied rhythm. No natural stopping points.

### Law 5 -- Sell the Feeling, Justify with Logic (Clayton Makepeace)
1. Identify the Dominant Resident Emotion (what they feel RIGHT NOW)
2. Amplify it
3. Present your product as the relief/solution
4. Stack proof so their logical brain can justify the emotional decision

### Law 6 -- The Persuasion Equation (Gary Bencivenga)
**Urgent Problem + Unique Promise + Unquestionable Proof + Irresistible Offer = Maximum Persuasion**
Apply the "Yeah Sure" test: after every claim, imagine the skeptic saying "Yeah, sure." If you can't answer with proof, rewrite.

### Law 7 -- Find the Starving Crowd First (Gary Halbert)
The single most important advantage is a STARVING CROWD. No amount of copy can sell steaks to vegetarians.

### Law 8 -- Test Everything, Assume Nothing (Claude Hopkins)
Advertising is salesmanship in print. No opinions -- only results. The copy with the best data wins.

## PART 2: HEADLINES

### The 4 U's (Dan Kennedy):
Every headline must score high on: Useful, Urgent, Ultra-specific, Unique

### Schwartz Headline Templates by Awareness:
- Most Aware: "[Product] -- Now [X]% Off for [Time]"
- Product Aware: "Why [Product] Is the #1 Choice for [Audience]"
- Solution Aware: "The [Mechanism] Method That [Result] Without [Pain]"
- Problem Aware: "Are You [Symptom]? The Hidden Cause Nobody Talks About"
- Unaware: "What [Authority] Discovered in [Place] That Changed Everything"

### Fascination Bullet Headlines (Gary Bencivenga / Parris Lampropoulos):
- "How a [unexpected thing] can [desired result]"
- "The [number] [thing] that [promise] -- (page [X])"
- "Why [common belief] is dead wrong -- and what to do instead"
- "What NEVER to [action] (unless you want [bad outcome])"

### Carlton's Sales Detective Headlines:
Find the ONE irresistible thing. Express it in the most specific, visceral language.
Formula: [Surprising Adjective] + [Secret/Discovery] + [Unlikely Character] + [Desired Result]

### Ogilvy's Rules:
- 5x more people read the headline than the body
- Include brand/product name
- Specifics beat generalities ("At 60 miles an hour...")
- News headlines work: "Introducing...", "New...", "Now..."

### David Deutsch's CopyTHINKING:
- Generate multiple Big Ideas BEFORE writing headlines
- Use unexpected juxtapositions: "How a pickpocket can cure your back pain"
- Write 3 complete drafts minimum

**Process:** Generate 25+ variations across 3+ formulas. Score on: (1) Curiosity, (2) Specificity, (3) Emotional pull, (4) Awareness match. Pick top 5.

## PART 3: VSL SCRIPTS

### The RMBC Method (Stefan Georgi -- $700M+ tracked sales)

**R -- Research (80% of the work):**
Study market exhaustively. Find the ONE unique mechanism. Document every proof element.

**M -- Mechanism:**
WHY your product works when everything else failed. Must be new or newly named, scientific but accessible, explain why previous solutions didn't work, create an "aha moment."

**B -- Brief (1-page):**
Who (avatar) + What (Big Promise) + Why (mechanism) + Proof (evidence stack)

**C -- Copy (this order):**
1. Hook (first 60s -- pattern interrupt + bold promise + curiosity)
2. Problem amplification (agitate + failed solutions)
3. Mechanism reveal (the "aha" moment)
4. Credibility
5. Benefits (future pace emotionally)
6. Proof stack (testimonials, data, demos)
7. Offer presentation (value stack)
8. Price anchor and reveal
9. Guarantee
10. CTA + urgency
11. Closing story (emotional callback)

### Jon Benson's 5-Step VSL Formula (Inventor of the VSL -- $12B+ format):
1. **Pattern Interrupt** -- bold claim in first 3 seconds
2. **Core Story** -- personal transformation (reluctant hero)
3. **Content/Education** -- teach something valuable
4. **Transition to Sale** -- "The reason I'm telling you this is..."
5. **The Close** -- value stack + price + guarantee + CTA

**NLP Techniques:** Presupposition ("When you start seeing results..."), Future Pacing ("Imagine 30 days from now..."), Embedded Commands, Pattern Interrupts every 2-3 minutes.

### VSL Technical Rules:
- Black text on white, key words RED or YELLOW
- One sentence per slide, max 2 lines
- 120-150 words per minute
- Visual cues: [SHOW: testimonial], [HIGHLIGHT: red]
- First 60 seconds hook or lose 80%

## PART 4: SALES LETTERS

### Gary Halbert's Architecture:
1. **A-Pile** -- look personal (handwritten font, stamps)
2. **Grabber** -- first sentence stops them cold
3. **Bridge** -- connect problem to solution through story
4. **Offer** -- stack value: core + bonuses + guarantee
5. **Close** -- urgency + scarcity + P.S. (most-read after headline)
6. **P.S.** -- minimum 2: restate main benefit + deadline

### John Carlton's Simple Writing System:
1. Research -- become the prospect
2. Find the USP
3. Write like you talk -- if you wouldn't say it in a bar, don't write it
4. Lead with the most outrageous truthful claim
5. Tell stories of transformation
6. The "So What?" test: after every sentence, if no benefit answer, cut it

### Four-Legged Stool (Clayton Makepeace):
1. **Big Promise** -- ONE transformational outcome
2. **Unique Mechanism** -- WHY it works
3. **Proof** -- stack evidence
4. **Irresistible Offer** -- saying "no" feels stupid

### The Big Marketing Idea (Todd Brown):
Formula: Emotionally Compelling + Primary Promise + Unique Mechanism + Intellectually Interesting
Without a Big Idea, your copy is just "another ad."

### Jim Rutz Anti-Bore Rules:
- #1 sin is being BORING. If reader predicts your next sentence, you lost them.
- Surprise on every page. Write to ONE person. Magalog format = editorial disguise.

## PART 5: LANDING PAGES

### Sugarman's Architecture:
1. Headline (stops scroll, creates curiosity)
2. Sub-headline (expands promise, qualifies reader)
3. Opening (max 8-word first sentence, builds momentum)
4. Problem (name their pain with specificity)
5. Agitation (consequences of not solving)
6. Solution introduction
7. Benefits stack (5-7 bullets, each a mini-promise)
8. Social proof (testimonials, logos, numbers)
9. Mechanism (HOW it works, simple)
10. Offer (what they get, value stack)
11. Guarantee (risk reversal)
12. CTA #1 (after benefits)
13. Objection handling (FAQ or narrative)
14. CTA #2 (after objections)
15. Final urgency (deadline, consequence of inaction)
16. CTA #3 (strongest language)

### Key Psychological Triggers (Sugarman):
Reciprocity, Consistency, Social Proof, Authority, Scarcity, Liking, Fear of Loss, Curiosity, Specificity, Storytelling, Belonging, Urgency, Exclusivity, Simplicity, Greed

### Rules:
- ONE conversion goal per page
- CTA minimum 3 times
- Button: "Get My [Result] Now" not "Submit"
- Mobile-first

## PART 6: EMAIL SEQUENCES

### Andre Chaperon's Soap Opera Sequence (SOS):

| Email | Purpose | Technique |
|-------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Set the Stage | Origin story + open loop |
| 2 | Backstory | Deepen story + empathy + cliffhanger |
| 3 | Epiphany | "Aha moment" + open loop |
| 4 | Hidden Benefits | Unexpected advantages + tease offer |
| 5 | Urgency & CTA | Close loops + present offer + deadline |

**Key:** Open loops, Sphere of Influence, write to ONE reader, story arcs. 60-83% open rates achievable.

### Ben Settle's Daily Email (Infotainment):
- ONE email/day, plain text, personality-first
- Entertain FIRST, sell SECOND
- Pop culture, contrarian opinions, polarization
- Every email links to offer -- selling embedded in story
- "Villains, Victims and Heroes" framework

### Ry Schwartz's CTC -- Launch Emails:
**5 Belief Shifts:**
1. "I need to do something" (Problem)
2. "This is the right approach" (Solution)
3. "This person can help me" (Authority)
4. "This is for someone like me" (Identity)
5. "Now is the time" (Urgency)

**Moment of Highest Tension (MOHT):** Open with hyper-specific scene.
Not "Are you stressed?" but "It's 2:47 AM and you're staring at the credit card bill..."

### Frank Kern -- Results In Advance:
Give genuine value BEFORE asking. Intent-Based Branding. Behavioral Dynamic Response (track clicks, send relevant follow-ups).

### Rules:
- Subject line = headline (use headline formulas)
- No "Hey [Name], hope you're well"
- One idea, one CTA per email
- P.S. = most-read after subject line
- Plain text > HTML for DR
- Dual subject lines for A/B testing

## PART 7: OFFERS

### Dan Kennedy's Architecture:
1. Core Product
2. Value Stack (bonuses for secondary problems)
3. Price Anchoring (total value then discounted price)
4. Risk Reversal (guarantee)
5. Urgency/Scarcity (logical, believable)
6. Payment Options

### Stack Formula (Russell Brunson):
"You get [Core] ($X) + [Bonus 1] ($Y) + [Bonus 2] ($Z) = Total $XXX. But today, just $X."

### Guarantee Types:
1. Unconditional -- money back, keep everything
2. Conditional -- do the work, if no result, money back
3. Performance -- work until you get [result]
4. Anti-guarantee -- "Not for everyone. No refunds."

### Offer Naming (David Deutsch / Todd Brown):
Proprietary name implying unique mechanism:
- "The 60-Year Rule" not "retirement planning guide"
- "The Gut Reset Protocol" not "digestive supplement"

## PART 8: BULLET POINTS / FASCINATIONS

### Master Templates:
1. **How To:** "How to [result] without [effort]"
2. **Secret:** "The secret of [admired thing] -- how you can [benefit]"
3. **Warning:** "WARNING: Never [action] without THIS (page X)"
4. **Number:** "The [number] [things] that [promise]"
5. **Why:** "Why [approach] almost never works -- and what to do instead"
6. **What:** "What NEVER to [action] after [trigger]"
7. **Unexpected:** "How a [unexpected thing] can [result]"
8. **Reversal:** "The #1 [thing] your [authority] won't tell you"
9. **Confession:** "The dirty secret the [industry] doesn't want you to know"
10. **Specific:** "[Number] ways to [result] -- #[X] will surprise you"

### Parris Lampropoulos Method:
Write 700-800 bullets from source material. Pick top 100. Top 3-5 become headlines/subject lines. Stanislavsky Method: emotionally BECOME the prospect. Format trumps copy.

## PART 9: AD COPY

### Dan Kennedy's Rules:
1. One step (low price = direct sale) or two step (high price = lead gen first)
2. Message-Market-Media match
3. Track EVERYTHING
4. 40-40-20 Rule: 40% list, 40% offer, 20% creative

### 5 Angles to Always Test:
1. **Pain** -- "Tired of [problem]?"
2. **Result** -- "How [person] got [result] in [time]"
3. **Curiosity** -- "The [adjective] [thing] that [claim]"
4. **Social proof** -- "[Number] people already [result]"
5. **Contrarian** -- "Stop [common advice]. Here's what works."

### Platform Rules:
- **Facebook:** Hook in 1-3 seconds. UGC > polished. Story to offer.
- **YouTube:** 5s hook before skip. 2-5 min for high-ticket.
- **Google:** Match search intent. Keywords in headline. Specific numbers.

## PART 10: FUNNEL COPY (Russell Brunson)

### Value Ladder:
Free (Lead Magnet) -> $7-47 (Tripwire) -> $97-497 (Core) -> $997-4997 (Premium) -> $10K+ (High Ticket)

### Hook-Story-Offer (HSO):
1. **Hook** -- stop them
2. **Story** -- hold them (origin, customer story, epiphany bridge)
3. **Offer** -- sell them (value stack, guarantee, urgency)

### Epiphany Bridge:
1. Backstory (before discovery)
2. Journey (what failed)
3. Epiphany moment
4. Transformation (after)
5. Framework (how others get same result)

### Funnel Types:
- **Opt-in:** Headline + bullets + email + CTA
- **Tripwire:** Thank you -> low-ticket -> OTO upsell
- **Webinar:** Registration -> confirmation -> reminders -> webinar -> replay -> close
- **VSL:** Ad -> VSL -> order -> upsell 1 -> upsell 2 -> thank you
- **Application:** Ad -> application -> calendar -> call -> close

### Dan Koe's One-Person Business Content:
- "The most profitable niche is YOU"
- Newsletter-first ecosystem: social -> newsletter -> product
- Short-form: hook within 3 words, one idea, strong POV

## PART 11: COPY CRITIQUE CHECKLIST

### Must-Pass (blocking):
- [ ] Headline creates curiosity, urgency, or bold promise
- [ ] Lead hooks within first two sentences
- [ ] Every sentence compels reading the next
- [ ] Offer is crystal clear
- [ ] CTA is specific, urgent, easy to follow
- [ ] Would YOU buy based on this copy?

### Should-Pass:
- [ ] Follows persuasion framework (PAS, AIDA, RMBC, HSO)
- [ ] Benefits > features
- [ ] Specific numbers (not vague claims)
- [ ] Social proof present
- [ ] Objections addressed
- [ ] Risk reversal (guarantee)
- [ ] Scarcity/urgency believable
- [ ] Audience's language
- [ ] Conversational tone
- [ ] Short paragraphs, varied rhythm
- [ ] Active voice
- [ ] Single objective
- [ ] Bullets = benefits
- [ ] Four-Legged Stool complete (Promise + Mechanism + Proof + Offer)
